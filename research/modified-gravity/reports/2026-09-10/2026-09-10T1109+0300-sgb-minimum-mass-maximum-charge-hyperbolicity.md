## Result

Rossi, Gualtieri and Sotiriou study static black holes in scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravity for a class of coupling functions that can formally support arbitrarily small masses. The physically relevant cutoff is not simply the existence of a background solution: below a model-dependent minimum mass the perturbation equations lose hyperbolicity and become elliptic, signalling breakdown of predictive evolution and, in the authors' interpretation, the effective theory's regime of validity.

The minimum mass can be pushed arbitrarily low by changing the coupling function. Crucially, however, doing so does **not** permit arbitrarily large departures from GR in the scalar sector: observable quantities such as the black-hole scalar charge remain bounded from above.

## Why this changes the picture

This separates two notions that are often conflated in strong-field modified-gravity scans: existence of very small black-hole solutions and availability of parametrically large observable deviations. A coupling can move the low-mass endpoint without buying an arbitrarily large scalar charge. Hyperbolicity therefore becomes a first-class viability cut before waveform/ringdown phenomenology, while scalar-charge saturation limits how much observational leverage can be gained merely by targeting smaller masses.

For the existing QuantDeus modified-gravity program, this complements the stored two-scale sGB/Galileon screening result: screening can suppress scalar charge/dipole emission, while the present result shows that even model engineering aimed at extending the small-mass branch cannot make scalar charge grow without bound. It also complements the stored high-spin QNM work by emphasizing that strong-field sensitivity must be evaluated inside the hyperbolic/EFT-valid domain.

## Caveats

- The result concerns the studied class of scalar–Gauss–Bonnet coupling functions and static black-hole backgrounds; it is not a universal theorem for every higher-curvature scalar-tensor theory.
- Loss of hyperbolicity is a sharp classical PDE pathology, but identifying it precisely with EFT breakdown is an interpretation rather than a direct UV-completion calculation.
- The paper does not by itself determine the best observable after imposing the scalar-charge upper bound; translating this into detector-level constraints remains a separate inference problem.
