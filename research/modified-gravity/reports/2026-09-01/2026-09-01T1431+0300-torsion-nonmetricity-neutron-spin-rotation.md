# Laboratory bounds on anisotropic torsion and nonmetricity from neutron spin rotation

**Accepted:** 2026-09-01 14:31 +03:00  
**Classification:** laboratory constraint / metric-affine modified gravity / torsion + nonmetricity / neutron spin rotation  
**Source quality:** primary arXiv preprint based on experimental neutron-spin-rotation data from Oak Ridge National Laboratory; submitted 2026-08-28; not yet peer-reviewed  
**Significance:** high for durable tracking; not promoted to Wix pending peer review / stronger external validation

## Development

Samiei, Lehnert, and Snow use neutron spin-rotation data from a compensated terbium iron garnet (TbIG) ferrimagnet to place the first experimental constraints on axially symmetric, direction-dependent in-matter torsion and nonmetricity combinations. The analysis is formulated as an effective field theory including neutron/electron couplings to torsion and nonmetricity through mass dimension six.

The key experimental observable is the neutron rotary power under reversal of the ferrimagnet electron-spin direction. The measured value,

`dphi/dL = [0.41 ± 6.30(stat.) ± 4.4(sys.)] × 10^-3 rad/m`,

is consistent with zero. Combining statistical and systematic uncertainties gives a 95% C.L. spin-energy sensitivity of `7.54 × 10^-24 GeV`.

Under single-sector assumptions, the work obtains

- `|(delta xi_0)_3| <= 7.54 × 10^-24 GeV` for the accessible anisotropic torsion combination;
- `|(delta zeta)_3| <= 7.54 × 10^-24 GeV` for the corresponding nonmetricity combination;
- `|(delta zeta_2)_3| <= 2.92 × 10^-13 GeV` for the beta^2-suppressed nonmetricity term.

The authors emphasize that these are the first laboratory constraints on anisotropic, spin-polarization-selected in-matter torsion and nonmetricity components. They also estimate that a dedicated intense slow-neutron experiment could improve sensitivity by two to three orders of magnitude.

## Why this clears the significance threshold

- This is a direct laboratory constraint on non-Riemannian connection degrees of freedom used in metric-affine and Einstein-Cartan-type modified-gravity frameworks, rather than a purely model-dependent black-hole or cosmological solution.
- The constrained components are qualitatively new relative to earlier isotropic in-matter searches: the polarized ferrimagnet selects an axis and exposes direction-dependent torsion/nonmetricity structures.
- The result establishes a concrete experimental falsification channel for modified-gravity sectors that couple spin-polarized matter to torsion or nonmetricity.
- The null result is quantitatively strong and experimentally grounded, while the authors provide a clear path to 10^2-10^3 sensitivity improvement.

## Relevance to Warp-bubble / metric-engineering research

Any warp or exotic-spacetime program that invokes metric-affine gravity, Einstein-Cartan extensions, nonmetricity-based gravity, or related connection degrees of freedom must satisfy not only background field equations and stability conditions, but also laboratory bounds on how those extra geometric fields couple to ordinary spin-polarized matter.

This result does not demonstrate a realizable warp solution and does not relax warp-drive energy requirements by itself. Its value is as a **hard laboratory filter**: candidate models that require appreciable torsion or nonmetricity in ordinary matter environments must remain compatible with these spin-rotation limits.

## Limits

- The result is a fresh arXiv preprint and has not yet completed peer review.
- The experiment measures specific EFT combinations rather than separately determining every torsion/nonmetricity tensor component.
- The interpretation assumes single-sector / single-coefficient limits when quoting individual sensitivities; cancellations among operators are possible.
- The minimal Einstein-Cartan scaling bound remains extremely weak compared with the nominal EC value, so the experiment does not tightly test minimal EC gravity itself.
