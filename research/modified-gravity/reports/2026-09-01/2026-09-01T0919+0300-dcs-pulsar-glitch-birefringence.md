# Dynamical Chern–Simons gravity: parity-violating GW strain from pulsar glitches

**Accepted:** 2026-09-01 09:19 +03:00  
**Classification:** propagation/strong-field test / dynamical Chern–Simons gravity / parity violation / neutron-star glitches / gravitational waves  
**Source quality:** primary arXiv preprint, arXiv:2608.24002 (submitted 2026-08-25; manuscript dated 2026-08-27). Not yet peer-reviewed.  
**Significance:** accepted for durable tracking; not promoted to public QuantDeus/Wix milestone because the result remains preliminary and detector viability is not yet established.

## Development

Abhishek Rout and Brett Altschul derive a parity-violating gravitational-wave signal from pulsar glitches in dynamical Chern–Simons (dCS) gravity. Using a slowly rotating Hartle–Thorne neutron-star background and a perturbative dCS coupling, they derive a modified Regge–Wheeler equation for axial perturbations and obtain a helicity-dependent phase shift between right- and left-circular GW polarizations.

The fractional polarization asymmetry scales as the square of the dCS coupling, grows linearly with GW frequency and stellar rotation rate, and falls steeply with stellar radius. In the constant-density model the relevant interior curvature source vanishes identically, so the scalar dipole is sourced by the exterior rotating spacetime; the authors argue that realistic central condensation can increase the signal relative to this conservative benchmark.

At the boundary of perturbative validity, their millisecond-pulsar benchmark reaches a fractional polarization asymmetry of order **10^-3 at kilohertz frequencies**. The authors emphasize, however, that practical inference is limited by the signal-to-noise ratio needed to resolve a ratio of GW strains, not merely by the formal size of the asymmetry.

## Why this clears the significance threshold

- It introduces a distinct compact-star transient channel for testing **gravitational parity violation** in a concrete modified-gravity theory.
- It provides an analytic/perturbative derivation from the dCS field equations through the stellar background to a detector-facing polarization observable.
- The predicted effect is quantitatively non-negligible in the optimistic edge of the EFT-valid regime, while the paper clearly states the observational bottleneck.
- It is non-duplicative with the repository's prior channels: merger memory, wave-optics lensing, EMRI orbital dephasing, and beyond-Horndeski ringdown/greybody factors.

## Deduplication / relation to prior accepted reports

Existing durable reports probe:

1. nonlinear source dynamics and gravitational memory in scalar–Gauss–Bonnet mergers;
2. propagation through lensing backgrounds in modified-gravity wave optics;
3. cumulative EMRI orbital dephasing from curvature-coupled scalar hair; and
4. effective gravitational horizons, ringdown, and greybody factors of beyond-Horndeski/DHOST primary-hair black holes.

This result instead probes **helicity-dependent GW propagation through the parity-violating scalar environment of a rotating neutron star during a transient glitch**. It therefore adds a new symmetry-sensitive observable rather than duplicating an existing strong-field channel.

## Limits

- The work is a fresh preprint and has not yet undergone peer review.
- The calculation uses slow rotation and perturbative dCS coupling; the largest quoted asymmetry occurs near the boundary of perturbative validity.
- The constant-density interior is an idealization, while realistic neutron-star structure requires EOS-dependent modeling.
- Individual glitch GW amplitudes are expected to be small, and resolving the polarization ratio requires high SNR.
- This is a forecast/theoretical signal model, not an observational detection or a new empirical bound on dCS gravity.

## Relevance to warp / exotic-spacetime research

The result reinforces a general design constraint for metric-engineering frameworks: extra geometric sectors can imprint not only background stress-energy changes but also **helicity- and polarization-dependent propagation effects**. Candidate warp-supporting theories with parity-odd or pseudoscalar curvature couplings should therefore be tested for polarization birefringence, characteristic-cone splitting, EFT validity, and detector-facing wave signatures alongside background energy conditions and stability.
