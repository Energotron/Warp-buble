# EMRIs as precision probes of curvature-coupled scalar hair

**Accepted:** 2026-08-31 03:21 UTC  
**Classification:** Strong-field test / scalar–Gauss–Bonnet + dynamical Chern–Simons / EMRI / LISA  
**Source quality:** Primary source, arXiv preprint (v1), submitted 2026-08-28. Not yet peer-reviewed.  
**Deduplication:** Distinct from prior accepted reports on scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravitational memory and wave-optics GW lensing: this result concerns strong-field orbital phasing and transition-to-plunge dynamics of EMRIs around curvature-coupled scalar-haired rotating black holes.

## Why this crosses the significance threshold

Leif Lui, Adrian Ka-Wai Chung, and Alejandro Torres-Orjuela model extreme-mass-ratio inspirals around rotating black holes carrying massive scalar hair generated by non-minimal coupling to curvature invariants. They analyze both scalar–Gauss–Bonnet (sGB) and dynamical Chern–Simons (dCS) couplings and propagate the resulting orbital deformations into effective gravitational-wave signals relevant to LISA.

For a representative $10^6 M_\odot$ primary with spin $a=0.8M$, mass ratio $10^{-4}$, and coupling $\zeta=10^{-3}$, the authors report accumulated orbital dephasings of about **+21.3 rad (dCS)** and **−45.2 rad (sGB)** over the final $\sim1.7\times10^3$ orbits. Even at $\zeta=10^{-4}$, the dephasing remains at the few-radian level. The sign difference between dCS and sGB also shifts the timing of plunge in opposite directions, giving a qualitative discriminant rather than only an amplitude change.

A multimode Fisher forecast for a LISA source with SNR $\sim80$ suggests that, in the idealized setup studied, the curvature-coupling parameter $\zeta$ could be measured to roughly **0.2%** and the scalar mass parameter $\mu$ to about **0.6–1.1%**. The corresponding multimode waveform mismatches relative to Kerr templates are far above the nominal detectability threshold in their benchmark cases.

## Physical interpretation

The non-minimal curvature coupling sources stationary scalar hair on the primary black hole. Backreaction deforms the exterior metric, shifting the ISCO and the strong-field orbital sequence. The signal is therefore produced mainly by **conservative strong-field geometry changes accumulated over many EMRI cycles**, with additional scalar-radiation corrections entering at higher order.

This gives modified gravity a detector-facing channel complementary to:

- source-side nonlinear gravitational-memory deviations in sGB mergers; and
- propagation-side wave-optics lensing effects.

The present result instead probes the **orbital dynamics of a compact object moving through a modified strong-field geometry**.

## Important limitations

The waveform treatment is not yet a fully self-consistent beyond-GR first-order calculation. The exact modified Teukolsky operator for generic spinning black holes is still unavailable, so the authors use the GR wave operator with a scalar-hair-shifted worldline to capture the leading morphology. They explicitly note this approximation and estimate the omitted wave-generation correction. The Fisher forecast also uses circular, equatorial benchmark EMRIs and fixes the black-hole spin, so quoted sub-percent precisions should be treated as optimistic model forecasts rather than observational bounds.

## Relevance to Warp-buble research

This strengthens the case for treating compact-object dynamics as a primary falsification channel for any curvature-coupled modified-gravity framework considered for metric engineering. If a candidate theory is invoked to alter effective stress-energy bookkeeping or permit unusual geometries, the same extra curvature couplings can generically shift geodesics, ISCO structure, plunge timing, and gravitational-wave phase. Those signatures can become constraints long before any direct metric-engineering experiment is conceivable.
