# Beyond-Horndeski primary-hair black holes: ringdown and greybody diagnostics

**Accepted:** 2026-08-31 10:27 +03:00  
**Classification:** Strong-field test / beyond-Horndeski-DHOST / primary scalar hair / black-hole ringdown  
**Primary source:** Georgios Antoniou, *Perturbations of black holes with primary hair: time evolutions, quasinormal modes and greybody factors*, arXiv:2608.26984v1 (submitted 2026-08-27). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26984

## Why this crosses the significance threshold

This work moves a recently constructed class of shift-symmetric beyond-Horndeski/DHOST black holes with genuine primary scalar hair from approximate perturbative estimates toward a substantially more complete strong-field response calculation. It directly evolves axial gravitational perturbations in the physical metric, independently solves the effective-metric master equation, computes quasinormal modes by direct boundary-value integration rather than relying only on WKB, and calculates gravitational greybody factors and absorption cross sections for the hairy backgrounds.

The result is distinct from the existing repository entries on scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravitational memory, modified-gravity wave-optics lensing, and EMRI dephasing. Here the new channel is the **ringdown/scattering response of a primary-hair beyond-Horndeski black hole itself**.

## Main findings

- Axial gravitational perturbations propagate on an effective geometry whose gravitational horizon can differ from the physical event horizon seen by matter and electromagnetic fields.
- The authors recover this effective gravitational horizon directly from the characteristic structure of the physical-metric perturbation equations, providing a nontrivial consistency check between the two descriptions.
- Time-domain waveforms and direct frequency-domain integration give compatible dominant quasinormal modes.
- Previous WKB estimates are validated at roughly sub-percent accuracy in the explored branch: the direct-integration vs sixth-order WKB deviation is about 0.5% for l=2 and decreases for higher multipoles.
- This is, to the author's knowledge, the first calculation of **gravitational-sector greybody factors** for these primary-hair black holes.
- Under localized small deformations of the effective potential, quasinormal frequencies can become highly sensitive while integrated greybody-factor observables remain perturbatively small, suggesting greybody factors may be a more robust strong-field diagnostic than QNM poles in this setting.

## Research significance

The important conceptual point is that modified gravity can split the causal/propagation geometry of gravitational perturbations from the physical metric experienced by ordinary matter. For metric-engineering studies this is a stronger constraint than simply finding a background solution: one must also track the characteristic cones, effective horizon, hyperbolicity, and perturbative observables of the gravitational sector.

The greybody-factor result adds a comparatively stable scattering observable to the modified-gravity ringdown toolkit, complementary to waveform phase, memory, and propagation effects already tracked in this repository.

## Limitations

- The analysis is restricted to axial perturbations of a specific asymptotically flat p=2 primary-hair branch.
- Polar perturbations, where scalar and gravitational sectors generally couple more strongly, remain for future work.
- The paper is a fresh arXiv preprint rather than a completed observational constraint from detector data.
