# Wave-optics gravitational-wave lensing in modified gravity

**Accepted:** 2026-08-26, *Physical Review D*  
**Authors:** Alice Garoffolo, Gianmassimo Tasinato  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1103/smzt-5v1s  
**Preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21584

## Classification

- **Type:** propagation test / gravitational-wave lensing / modified gravity
- **Evidence level:** peer-reviewed accepted paper; phenomenological theoretical framework
- **Novelty:** first dedicated treatment of gravitational-wave lensing in the wave-optics regime within modified gravity
- **Significance:** high enough for durable tracking because it opens an observational channel that can remain invisible in geometric optics

## Core result

Garoffolo and Tasinato study gravitational-wave propagation with a curvature-coupled amplitude equation. The setup reproduces the general-relativistic geometric-optics limit, but the infrared wave-optics regime changes qualitatively.

In GR, the lensing amplification factor tends to unity toward zero frequency. In the modified-gravity setup considered here, curvature-induced interactions persist in the infrared, so this limit no longer follows. The natural propagation basis becomes dressed by the curvature interaction, standard Fresnel treatment fails at sufficiently low frequency, and the infrared regime is instead described by an interacting static Green function with a partial-wave finite-frequency completion.

The authors also provide a distorted-wave interpretation: curvature effects are absorbed into a dressed reference propagation basis, while residual lensing appears as finite-frequency phase shifts. This establishes a clean route for probing propagation-level departures from GR through wave-optics lensing.

## Why this is distinct from prior accepted results

The previous durable report tracked scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravitational-wave memory generated by strong-field binary dynamics. This result is not a duplicate:

- **Prior report:** nonlinear source/merger dynamics; memory observable; scalar–Gauss–Bonnet model.
- **This report:** propagation through curved/lensing backgrounds; low-frequency wave optics; phenomenological curvature-coupled propagation.

The two effects occupy different stages of the signal chain and therefore provide complementary tests of modified gravity.

## Relevance to Warp-bubble research

This result is relevant because it demonstrates that modified gravity can alter how perturbations propagate through curved backgrounds even when the geometric-optics limit looks GR-like. For exotic metrics, this motivates separating two questions:

1. whether the background geometry can be supported by a modified field sector, and
2. whether perturbations propagating on that background acquire characteristic low-frequency phase/amplitude effects.

That second channel may provide testable signatures for any candidate modified-gravity warp geometry, rather than relying only on background energy-condition bookkeeping.

## Limitations

- The study is phenomenological rather than a survey of a specific complete modified-gravity theory.
- The observable size of the effect is model- and lens-dependent.
- Detectability forecasts for concrete detector/lens populations remain to be established.
- The result does not by itself relax warp-drive energy conditions or demonstrate a realizable warp solution.

## Significance decision

**ACCEPTED.** The paper establishes a qualitatively new gravitational-wave observable for modified gravity, has peer-reviewed acceptance in *Physical Review D*, and is non-duplicative with the existing strong-field memory report.
