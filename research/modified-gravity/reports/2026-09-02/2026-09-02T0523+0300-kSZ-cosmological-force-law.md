# Cosmological-scale inverse-square gravity test with kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich velocities

**Accepted:** 2026-09-02 05:23 +03:00  
**Classification:** Cosmological observational constraint / force-law test / kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect / ACT + SDSS  
**Source quality:** Peer-reviewed primary literature — *Physical Review Letters* 136, 151002 (published 2026-04-15); recently highlighted publicly in August 2026  
**Significance:** high

## Development

Gallardo et al. use the mean pairwise velocity of massive halos as a direct probe of the radial gravitational force law on cosmological scales. They combine Atacama Cosmology Telescope CMB intensity maps with a Sloan Digital Sky Survey galaxy catalog and infer the pairwise-velocity signal through the kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (kSZ) effect.

Across separations of **30–230 Mpc**, they fit a phenomenological acceleration law

`g(r) ∝ 1 / r^n`

and obtain **n = 2.1 ± 0.3**, consistent with the inverse-square behavior expected from Newtonian gravity embedded in standard expanding-space cosmology. The result establishes kSZ pairwise velocities as an observational gravity test across distances far larger than galaxy or cluster internal scales.

## Why this clears the significance threshold

- It is a peer-reviewed primary observational result in *Physical Review Letters*.
- It is a direct empirical constraint on the radial force law rather than a model-only forecast.
- The measurement probes gravity over **30–230 Mpc**, adding a scale regime not represented by the existing strong-field, propagation, laboratory, or theory-consistency reports.
- It disfavors large departures from inverse-square behavior in this regime and therefore narrows the space available to modified-gravity explanations that rely on a substantially shallower long-range force law.
- The observable has a clear upgrade path: the authors forecast that upcoming surveys can sharply improve discrimination, including strong rejection power against an `n = 1` scaling.

## Deduplication / comparison with prior accepted results

This is distinct from the existing cosmological non-minimal gravity–matter coupling entry. That report concerns Bayesian preference for a specific modified-gravity cosmological model and an inferred time evolution of effective gravitational strength. The present result instead measures the **spatial radial scaling of the gravitational force law** from halo peculiar velocities, without relying on the same model comparison.

It is also independent of the repository's strong-field black-hole, EMRI, ringdown, gravitational-memory, parity-violation, laboratory torsion/nonmetricity, and UV-locality channels.

## Relevance to Warp / exotic-spacetime research

For metric-engineering work, the result is primarily a boundary-condition and phenomenology constraint. Any modified-gravity framework proposed to support unusual local geometries must reduce to an effectively inverse-square force law on cosmological halo-separation scales unless it contains screening, scale dependence, environmental dependence, or another mechanism that preserves this observed regime.

The measurement does **not** directly rule out higher-curvature, scalar-tensor, parity-violating, or screened theories whose departures are confined to other scales or environments. Its value for the Warp Bubble Lab is therefore as a large-scale consistency gate rather than as a direct test of warp-capable geometries.

## Limits

- The fitted `n` is a phenomenological force-law exponent; it is not a complete model-independent constraint on every modified-gravity theory.
- The result is consistent with `n = 2` but has uncertainty large enough that moderate departures remain allowed.
- The analysis is performed within an expanding cosmological context and depends on the halo-velocity inference from kSZ statistics.
- It does not directly probe strong-field gravity or local exotic-spacetime solutions.
