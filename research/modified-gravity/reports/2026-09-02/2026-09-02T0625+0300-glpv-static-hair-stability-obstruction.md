# General GLPV static scalar hair: parity-complete stability conditions and near-horizon obstruction

**Accepted:** 2026-09-02 06:25 +03:00  
**Classification:** Strong-field theoretical consistency / GLPV-beyond-Horndeski / static scalar hair / black-hole stability  
**Source quality:** Primary source, arXiv v1, submitted 2026-09-01; not yet peer-reviewed.  
**Primary source:** Petarpa Boonserm, Antonio De Felice, Ratchaphat Nakarachinda, Shinji Tsujikawa, Pitayuth Wongjun, *Perturbations and stability of black holes with static scalar hair in general GLPV theories*, arXiv:2609.00664v1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00664

## Development

The authors derive the background equations and complete odd- and even-parity quadratic actions for static, spherically symmetric black holes with radial scalar hair in general quartic-quintic GLPV theories, including Horndeski. On regular nondegenerate branches the formulation remains valid across Killing horizons and yields local no-ghost conditions together with radial and angular eikonal characteristics.

A key structural result is that requiring both even-parity angular branches to retain finite, nonzero eikonal phase speeds selects a compatibility condition linking the quartic and quintic beyond-Horndeski functions. When the corresponding disformal map is regular and invertible, this condition aligns the relevant degeneracy directions with a Horndeski-related branch.

The strongest result is a near-horizon obstruction: for a finite, nonzero scalar kinetic term at a simple outer horizon, every nontrivial branch of the exact quadratic GLPV black hole is locally unstable or develops a degenerate tensor cone. The authors argue that, apart from identified exceptions, the obstruction extends to general shift- and reflection-symmetric quadratic GLPV theories and to the regular Horndeski-related quartic-quintic branch.

They also identify an important exception class. For scalar-Gauss-Bonnet black holes whose scalar kinetic term vanishes at the horizon, analytic power-law quartic beyond-Horndeski deformations can satisfy the same compatibility condition and, at sufficiently small coupling, obey all local no-ghost and high-frequency gradient-stability conditions throughout the exterior.

## Why this crosses the significance threshold

- It supplies a parity-complete perturbative treatment rather than only a background solution or a single axial sector.
- It establishes a broad near-horizon no-go/degeneracy result for nontrivial static scalar-hair branches in GLPV gravity.
- It identifies a concrete structural compatibility condition between quartic and quintic beyond-Horndeski functions and connects it to a regular disformal relation with Horndeski.
- It also isolates a viable exception family, preventing the result from being merely a blanket exclusion.

## Deduplication / comparison with prior accepted reports

This is distinct from the repository's prior beyond-Horndeski/DHOST primary-hair ringdown report. That work studied axial perturbations, quasinormal modes, an effective gravitational horizon, and greybody factors for a specific primary-hair branch. The present result is broader and complementary: it derives both parity sectors for general GLPV static radial hair and exposes a generic near-horizon instability/degenerate-cone obstruction.

It is also distinct from the accepted cubic-Galileon cosmological-hair report, which established stable black-hole solutions matched to a cosmological scalar background. Here the new contribution is a local parity-complete strong-field viability gate for static GLPV hair.

## Relevance to Warp-bubble / metric-engineering research

For modified-gravity metric engineering, this strengthens the validation pipeline. A formally acceptable background metric is insufficient: candidate scalar-tensor constructions must also pass full odd/even quadratic-action checks, horizon regularity, no-ghost conditions, finite characteristic speeds, and nondegenerate tensor cones. The GLPV result shows that apparently healthy static hairy backgrounds can fail precisely at this perturbative level near horizons.

## Limitations

- Fresh arXiv preprint; not yet peer-reviewed.
- The no-go statement is local to the analyzed static, spherically symmetric radial-hair setup and the specified regularity assumptions.
- The stable scalar-Gauss-Bonnet deformation branch is established perturbatively at sufficiently small coupling, not as a general nonlinear completion.
- The result is a theoretical consistency milestone rather than an observational detection or bound.
