# Non-minimally coupled gravity as a joint fit to major cosmological tensions

**Accepted:** 2026-09-02 02:20 +03:00  
**Classification:** Cosmological observational test / non-minimal gravity–matter coupling / Horndeski-EFT / DESI + CMB + SNe  
**Source quality:** Peer-reviewed primary literature — *Physical Review D*, accepted 14 July 2026; DOI 10.1103/2zc8-m8s8  
**Significance:** High

## Development

Gen Ye reports a scalar-tensor / non-minimally coupled-gravity fit in which several major late- and early-Universe tensions are treated as consequences of a common departure from general relativity rather than as independent anomalies. In the analysis summarized by *Physical Review D*, Bayesian model comparison favors the non-minimally coupled model over GR/ΛCDM with **ln B = +12.1** for the reported data combination.

The model predicts an effective gravitational strength on cosmological scales before recombination that is approximately **5% larger than today**. This is a falsifiable prediction rather than only a phenomenological fit: the inferred shift in the early-time gravitational coupling is stated to be compatible with present constraints and testable with future CMB and big-bang-nucleosynthesis measurements.

## Why this crosses the significance threshold

- The source is a peer-reviewed primary paper accepted by *Physical Review D*.
- The result is **observationally data-facing**, not merely a formal construction of an alternative theory.
- The reported Bayes factor, ln B = +12.1, is materially stronger than the earlier DESI-era hints for non-minimal coupling.
- The model makes a concrete independent prediction: an early-Universe gravitational coupling about 5% stronger than the present value.
- The result adds a new repository channel: cosmological inference of the gravitational coupling itself, complementary to strong-field, propagation, laboratory, and theoretical-consistency tests already tracked here.

## Deduplication / comparison with prior accepted results

No existing report in `research/modified-gravity/` covers a cosmological Bayesian preference for non-minimal gravity–matter coupling derived from the combined cosmological-tension problem.

The closest conceptual predecessor outside this repository is the 2025 *Physical Review Letters* result reporting a DESI-based hint of non-minimally coupled gravity and a stable phantom-divide crossing. The present result is accepted because it materially strengthens that line: it jointly addresses multiple cosmological tensions, reports a substantially stronger Bayesian preference in the stated analysis, and produces a specific prediction for the relative early-time gravitational strength.

It is distinct from the repository's cubic-Galileon cosmology-to-strong-field report, which establishes theoretical viability of stable cosmological scalar hair around black holes rather than inferring a modified gravitational coupling from precision cosmological data.

## Interpretation

The result points toward a useful consistency requirement for modified-gravity programs: candidate theories should not be assessed only against local or strong-field constraints. If the same extra sector changes the effective Planck mass or gravitational coupling on cosmological backgrounds, its time evolution must remain compatible with CMB, BAO, supernova, structure-growth, BBN, and local-gravity limits simultaneously.

For metric-engineering / warp-oriented work, this does **not** demonstrate a warp-capable mechanism. Its relevance is as a global boundary-condition and coupling-evolution constraint on scalar-tensor frameworks that might otherwise be invoked to alter effective stress-energy or curvature response locally.

## Limitations

- The evidence is model- and dataset-dependent; the quoted Bayes factor is not a direct laboratory or astrophysical detection of modified gravity.
- Cosmological-tension inferences are sensitive to data combinations, calibration choices, parameterization, and priors.
- The approximately 5% enhancement is an inferred cosmological-scale effective coupling, not a statement that Newton's constant is universally 5% larger in all environments.
- Independent replication with future CMB, BBN, DESI and large-scale-structure data remains essential.
