# Stable black holes with cosmological scalar hair in cubic Galileon gravity

**Accepted:** 2026-09-02 01:23 +03:00  
**Classification:** Strong-field/cosmology bridge / cubic Galileon / scalar-tensor gravity / stable cosmological hair  
**Source quality:** Peer-reviewed primary literature — *Physical Review D* 114, 044072 (published 2026-08-24)  
**Significance:** high

## Development

Laurens Smulders and Johannes Noller construct black-hole solutions in the cubic Galileon that simultaneously retain the required long-range cosmological behavior of a time-dependent dark-energy scalar and remain regular and stable in the short-range strong-field region around the black hole. This addresses a central failure mode of earlier cosmological-hair solutions, which were known to suffer from instabilities.

The key conceptual result is that the local scalar hair can encode information about the cosmological scalar background. In this class of scalar-tensor theories, black-hole strong-field structure is therefore not completely separable from late-time cosmological dynamics: the cosmological field can leave a stable, local imprint rather than being screened away or rendered pathological.

## Deduplication / comparison with prior accepted reports

The closest existing entry is the beyond-Horndeski/DHOST primary-hair ringdown report, but that work concerns perturbations, effective gravitational horizons, quasinormal modes, and greybody factors of a specific asymptotically flat primary-hair branch. The present result instead concerns **cosmologically sourced scalar hair** in the cubic Galileon and demonstrates simultaneous **long-range cosmological matching + local regularity + stability**.

It also differs from the scalar–Gauss–Bonnet and dCS EMRI/memory entries: those test curvature-coupled scalar hair through gravitational-wave observables, whereas this result first establishes the viability of a stable cosmology-to-black-hole scalar-hair connection itself.

## Relevance to warp / exotic-spacetime research

For modified-gravity metric engineering, the result strengthens a general requirement: candidate exotic geometries cannot be assessed only as isolated local solutions. If the supporting scalar sector has cosmological boundary data, that data can feed into local strong-field structure. Viability checks should therefore include global-to-local matching, regularity, ghost/gradient stability, and the dependence of local observables on asymptotic cosmological scalar evolution.

This does **not** demonstrate a warp solution or relax warp-drive energy requirements. Its relevance is as a consistency and model-building precedent showing that scalar-tensor theories can support nontrivial, stable strong-field hair tied to cosmological boundary conditions.

## Limits

- The analysis focuses on the cubic Galileon as a concrete illustrative theory, not the full Horndeski/DHOST landscape.
- The result is theoretical; it does not constitute an observational detection of cosmological scalar hair.
- Concrete detector forecasts for extracting the cosmological information encoded in the hair remain future work.
- The existence of stable cosmological hair does not imply viability of arbitrary exotic metrics.
