# Galileon EFT nonlinear evolution: regularization, well-posedness, and screening

- **Observed:** 2026-09-07 11:02 +03:00
- **Source date:** 2026-09-04
- **Classification:** nonlinear simulation / theoretical consistency / Galileon EFT / Vainshtein screening / well-posedness
- **Source quality:** high-quality fresh arXiv preprint from established modified-gravity / numerical-gravity groups; not yet peer-reviewed
- **Primary source:** arXiv:2609.05407, *Nonlinear evolution in Galileon EFTs: Regularization and screening* (F. Thaalba, A. Held, R. Cayuso, T. P. Sotiriou)

## Significant result

The paper evolves spherically symmetric Galileon effective field theories nonlinearly and cleanly separates the derivative expansion from the strong-field expansion. For initial data that remain within the derivative-expansion regime, the ghost-free Galileon develops ill-posed regions only when the derivative expansion itself breaks down, including in a strong-field regime. This sharpens the interpretation of apparent PDE pathologies: they are not automatically evidence that the low-energy theory fails before its EFT validity bound.

A higher-derivative ghost term can regularize the equations and reproduce the ghost-free evolution while that evolution remains well-posed, but it becomes tachyonic when the unregularized system crosses into its ill-posed regime. More importantly for screened modified gravity, in the Vainshtein-like screening regime the regularizing ghost term naturally dominates over the Galileon interaction, contrary to the usual assumption that such higher-derivative operators stay parametrically subdominant.

## Why it matters

This is a direct nonlinear consistency result for a flagship modified-gravity mechanism. It affects how numerical strong-field predictions in Galileon/Horndeski-like EFTs should be interpreted and constrains common regularization strategies used to extend evolutions through problematic regions. In particular, successful screening does not by itself guarantee that omitted higher-derivative operators remain negligible.

For the Warp-buble / QuantDeus research roadmap, the result raises the bar for any nonlinear modified-gravity or warp-adjacent simulation: track derivative-expansion validity separately from field amplitude, and treat higher-derivative regularization as a possible change of regime rather than an innocuous numerical fix.
