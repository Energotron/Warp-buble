# Quadratic f(R): screened scalar hair closes weak lensing as the leading compact-object discriminant

- **Observed:** 2026-09-07 20:12 +03:00
- **Source date:** 2026-09-03
- **Classification:** theory / model / quadratic f(R) gravity / screened scalar hair / compact objects / gravitational lensing
- **Source quality:** primary arXiv preprint with an explicit analytic derivation plus direct-quadrature comparison; not yet peer-reviewed
- **Primary source:** arXiv:2609.04419, *Screened Scalar Hair and the Weak-Lensing Separation of Black Holes from Neutron Stars in Quadratic f(R) Gravity* (Yashmitha Kumaran, Ilídio Lopes)
- **Primary URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04419

## Significant result

For quadratic f(R) gravity, the extra massive scalar degree of freedom has finite range λ and produces a Yukawa-suppressed exterior correction rather than a polynomial correction in the coupling. The analysis derives an intrinsically isotropic exterior and shows that the scalaron's leading contribution cancels in the light-deflection combination: the leading weak-lensing angle remains exactly the GR value 4GM/b.

The first non-GR correction is exponentially screened, with the reported scaling λ^(-1/2) b^(-3/2) exp(-b/λ). The authors compare the approximation against direct quadrature, reporting roughly 20% agreement at b=4λ and about 7% at b=12λ.

The compact-object distinction survives through scalar charge rather than ordinary large-impact-parameter bending: a static black hole has no scalar hair in this setup, while a neutron star can acquire a pressure-weighted scalar charge fixed by its interior structure and equation of state.

## Why it changes the picture

This is more than another small f(R) correction. It removes an apparently straightforward observational channel: in this screened quadratic model, weak lensing at large impact parameter is parametrically poor and its leading term is exactly degenerate with GR even when a material compact object carries scalar charge.

The practical target therefore shifts toward strong-field photon-sphere imaging and neutron-star interior/EoS-sensitive observables. For the modified-gravity watch, this is a useful model-selection and experiment-design constraint: the existence of an extra scalar degree of freedom does not imply an observable leading-order weak-lensing deviation, especially when the exterior correction is non-analytic in the coupling and Yukawa screened.

For warp/exotic-spacetime work, the relevance is indirect but important: screening can hide modified gravitational structure from asymptotic optical tests. Any proposal that relies on far-field lensing as a generic diagnostic of a modified metric should verify the observable combination explicitly instead of inferring detectability from a non-GR metric component alone.

## Status

**Accepted as significant with caution.** Classification is theory/model. The result is primary-source and analytically explicit, but it is a fresh arXiv preprint, model-specific, and not yet peer-reviewed. The quoted asymptotic correction is only moderately accurate close to a few scalaron ranges, so quantitative forecasts should use direct integration in that regime.
