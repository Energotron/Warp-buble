# Cosmological consistency confines symmetry-breaking ESGB hair to gram-scale primordial black holes

**Accepted:** 2026-09-03 05:21 +03:00  
**Classification:** Theoretical constraint / Einstein–scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravity / cosmological consistency / hairy primordial black holes  
**Source quality:** Primary technical preprint — arXiv:2609.02102v1 (submitted 2026-09-02)  
**Significance:** high

## Development

Suruj Jyoti Das and Miok Park test whether stable black-hole hair generated by spontaneous symmetry breaking in Einstein–scalar–Gauss–Bonnet (ESGB) gravity can be embedded consistently in cosmological history. Their model uses a complex scalar with global U(1) symmetry, quadratic and quartic scalar couplings to the Gauss–Bonnet invariant, and a bare quartic self-interaction.

The key obstruction comes from the sign of the FLRW Gauss–Bonnet invariant. It is positive during inflation but becomes negative during post-inflationary decelerated expansion. In the authors' setup this temporarily makes the effective scalar potential unbounded from below and opens a tachyonic instability band. Requiring scalar perturbations not to grow enough to spoil the standard cosmological evolution sharply restricts the interaction scale and the mass of black holes that can scalarize.

For the viable benchmark region, the cutoff associated with the scalar–Gauss–Bonnet operators must lie near

```text
Λ ≈ 10^-5 m_P
```

and the resulting scalar-hairy black holes are confined to approximately

```text
M_BH ≈ 1–10 g.
```

The black-hole scalarization condition is

```text
α / Λ_2^2 ≳ (5/6) G^2 M_BH^2,
```

so pushing the cutoff high enough to control the cosmological instability simultaneously pushes scalarization down to ultralight masses. The authors construct a representative hairy solution near 10 g and find that its scalar-test-field greybody factors differ from Schwarzschild by less than order unity, with the larger deviations at low multipoles.

## Why this clears the significance threshold

- It supplies a quantitative global consistency constraint, not merely another local hairy metric or parameter scan.
- It links early-Universe stability directly to the mass scale at which ESGB scalar hair can exist.
- In this symmetry-breaking model, astrophysical stellar-mass hair is effectively excluded by the assumed cosmological history; the surviving objects are gram-scale primordial black holes.
- It provides a strong theory-level filter for any proposal that uses curvature-induced scalar structure locally while assuming a standard cosmological embedding.

## Deduplication / comparison with prior accepted reports

The closest accepted report is **“Stable black holes with cosmological scalar hair in cubic Galileon gravity.”** That peer-reviewed construction shows that cosmologically sourced scalar hair can remain regular and stable around black holes in a cubic-Galileon branch.

The present ESGB result is distinct and complementary: it follows a global U(1) symmetry-breaking scalar–Gauss–Bonnet mechanism through inflation and the subsequent decelerating epoch, where the sign flip of the cosmological Gauss–Bonnet invariant creates a tachyonic consistency problem. Rather than establishing generic viability, it imposes a severe model-specific veto that compresses the allowed hairy-black-hole mass window to roughly 1–10 g.

It is also not a duplicate of the accepted scalar–Gauss–Bonnet GW-memory or EMRI reports. Those quantify signals from assumed curvature-coupled hair; this work tests whether the underlying hairy objects can exist consistently across cosmological history.

## Relevance to warp / exotic-spacetime research

Any metric-engineering proposal supported by curvature-coupled scalar sectors must be evolved through the relevant cosmological background, not validated only near the engineered geometry. A local solution can be regular and perturbatively stable yet fail because the same couplings destabilize the early-Universe scalar sector.

The corresponding validation gate is:

```text
local scalarized geometry
→ EFT cutoff hierarchy
→ inflationary vacuum
→ post-inflationary sign changes
→ tachyonic-mode growth
→ backreaction on cosmology
→ surviving mass/curvature window
```

This paper does not construct a warp metric or reduce exotic-energy requirements. Its importance is the demonstrated possibility that global cosmological consistency can eliminate an otherwise viable-looking local curvature-coupled branch.

## Confidence and limits

- **Classification:** theoretical constraint / model construction, not an observation.
- The result is a fresh arXiv v1 and has not yet been peer reviewed.
- The strong mass restriction applies to the specific complex-scalar ESGB action, assumed global U(1) symmetry, coupling truncation, self-interaction, inflationary scale, and post-inflationary history used in the paper; it is not a no-go theorem for all scalar–Gauss–Bonnet theories.
- The action is treated as an EFT rather than a demonstrated UV completion, with the hierarchy `Λ < Λ_UV` assumed.
- The near-black-hole/cosmology separation is estimated; for the gram-scale benchmark the Schwarzschild approximation is reported valid only out to roughly `10^2–10^3` Schwarzschild radii as the Universe expands.
- Such ultralight primordial black holes would evaporate well before BBN. A full Hawking-radiation and cosmological-signature calculation is left for future work.
- The greybody calculation uses a massless scalar test field and does not by itself establish an observable surviving signal.
