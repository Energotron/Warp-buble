## Core result

The paper studies an entropic-gravity construction in which the usual equipartition relation on the holographic screen is modified by a temperature-dependent correction function. Solving the resulting modified Einstein equations for static, spherically symmetric spacetimes produces two structurally useful conclusions:

1. If the temperature correction scales exactly as `f(T) ∝ T^2`, the corresponding spacetime collapses to the flat Minkowski metric rather than producing a nontrivial Schwarzschild-like gravitational field.
2. To obtain a small but nonzero gravitational field, the correction law must depart from exact quadratic scaling. In that nearby branch, the effective weak-field potential acquires a logarithmic correction to the Newtonian form.
3. Because logarithmic terms fall off more slowly than `1/r`, their relative importance increases in very weak-field / large-radius regimes, making orbital and lensing phenomenology the natural place to constrain the branch.

## Why this changes the picture

This adds a different kind of viability filter to the repository's recent modified-gravity results. The key issue is not only ghost freedom, hyperbolicity, constraint closure, or GW propagation: in entropic/emergent constructions, the microscopic thermodynamic response law itself can sit on a **degenerate flat-spacetime locus**. A seemingly natural correction (`T^2`) does not merely make gravity small; it removes the static gravitational field entirely.

That matters for warp-adjacent model building because any proposal that tries to engineer effective geometry through thermodynamic / entropic response must first verify that its constitutive law actually supports a nontrivial local metric before energy-condition engineering or bubble kinematics are considered.

The logarithmic weak-field correction is also phenomenologically important. It provides a long-range observable that can be tested against Solar-System dynamics, wide binaries, galactic rotation curves, lensing, and cosmological weak-field constraints. This makes the model more falsifiable than a purely formal modification of the field equations.

## Scope / caveats

This is a static, spherically symmetric analysis. It does not establish dynamical stability, cosmological viability, gravitational-wave propagation, or a warp solution. The logarithmic correction must be confronted with precision weak-field bounds before the branch can be considered broadly viable.
