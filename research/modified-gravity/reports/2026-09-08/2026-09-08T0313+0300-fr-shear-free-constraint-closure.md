# Metric f(R) shear-free cosmologies: constraint closure removes ordinary tensor GWs

- **Accepted:** 2026-09-08 03:13 +03:00
- **Classification:** theory / model-consistency / metric f(R) gravity / cosmological perturbations / gravitational-wave propagation
- **Primary source:** arXiv:2609.03260v1, *Constraint closure and gravitational-wave content of shear-free cosmologies in metric f(R) gravity* (submitted 2026-09-03)
- **Source quality:** primary technical preprint with explicit covariant perturbation equations, spatial/temporal constraint propagation, harmonic-sector decomposition, background viability audits, and a stated classification theorem; not yet peer reviewed.
- **Status:** accepted as significant and non-duplicate relative to the current modified-gravity index/checkpoint.

## Core result

For linear perturbations of FLRW solutions in metric `f(R)` gravity, imposing and propagating exact shear-freeness of the chosen timelike matter congruence is substantially more restrictive than merely solving a formal wave equation.

The analysis finds:

1. The transverse-traceless electric/magnetic Weyl pair vanishes in the exactly shear-free sector, so the ordinary `+` and `×` tensor gravitational-wave polarizations do not survive there.
2. The scalaron is not removed kinematically, but admissible scalar harmonics must remain inside the time-dependent subspace generated by the shear-free constraint and all of its temporal derivatives.
3. In the geodesic, spatially flat, expanding de Sitter patch, this closure excludes every nonzero fixed comoving scalar harmonic, including the healthy `R + αR² - 2Λ` model with `α, Λ > 0`.
4. For positive-density barotropic matter with `f'(R) > 0` and `1+w > 0`, the vector-sector global harmonic condition has the wrong sign, excluding nonzero regular vector harmonics on that branch.
5. Earlier coasting `R^n` rotating/expanding examples evade the sign obstruction only on unhealthy branches with `f' < 0` or `f'' < 0`.

## Why this changes the picture

This sharpens model-selection logic for modified gravity: a formal second-order wave equation is not sufficient evidence for a physical propagating degree of freedom once kinematical constraints are imposed. Constraint closure, global harmonic admissibility, and background viability must be checked together.

For warp-relevant gravity work, this reinforces a broader screening rule already emerging in the repository: exotic or highly constrained geometries must be tested for whether their perturbative degrees of freedom remain dynamically and globally admissible, not merely whether the background metric solves modified field equations.

The result is distinct from the already accepted quadratic-`f(R)` screened-lensing cancellation. That earlier result concerned weak-lensing observability of screened scalar hair around compact objects; this result concerns the existence and propagation of tensor/scalar/vector perturbative sectors under an exactly shear-free cosmological constraint.

## Scope / caveats

The theorem is restricted to linear perturbations about FLRW backgrounds in metric `f(R)` gravity with the specified minimally coupled barotropic perfect-fluid setup and an imposed shear-free timelike congruence. It is not a no-go theorem for general non-shear-free `f(R)` cosmologies, nonlinear waves, anisotropic backgrounds, shear-free null congruences, or arbitrary imperfect matter.
