## Core result
The paper shows that a sixth-order higher-gradient modification of the Newtonian field equation, which would carry an Ostrogradsky instability under the obvious higher-derivative Lagrangian dynamics, can instead be obtained as the stationary limit of a first-order thermodynamic relaxation law. In that construction the gravitational potential is a thermodynamic variable, the evolution is a gradient flow, and stability is controlled by entropy concavity; there is no propagating higher-derivative ghost mode in the classical relaxation dynamics.

The derivation also fixes the gravitational pressure tensor and energy current, reproduces Newton's force law through an exact identity, and permits a nondissipative cross-coupling to bulk viscous pressure that is not generated by a standard variational principle.

For the stationary point-mass solution, regularity enforces the Yukawa-amplitude sum rule `Σ_i α_i = -1`. Existing short-range inverse-square-law tests then bound the largest internal length scale below about `4×10^-5 m`. The paper also identifies a complex-root sector that is classically stable in the thermodynamic formulation and is analogous, at the level of spatial response, to a Lee-Wick-like regime.

## Why this changes the picture
This adds a distinct consistency branch for modified gravity: a higher-gradient *spatial* field equation need not inherit the ghost content of the corresponding higher-time-derivative Lagrangian completion if the dynamics is instead defined by irreversible/thermodynamic relaxation. For warp-adjacent model selection, this means "higher gradients imply Ostrogradsky ghost" is not a universally valid rejection rule; one must first identify the actual dynamical completion and its characteristic/time-evolution structure.

The result does **not** provide a relativistic warp metric or a relativistic ghost-free higher-curvature theory. Its value is methodological: it supplies a concrete counterexample in the Newtonian sector and a laboratory-constrained consistency template that a relativistic extension would have to reproduce without sacrificing causality, covariance, or hyperbolicity.
