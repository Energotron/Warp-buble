# Two-scale scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravity: screened dipolar emission

- **Run time:** 2026-09-09 12:11 +03:00
- **Classification:** theory / model / strong-field modified gravity / scalar–Gauss–Bonnet / cubic Galileon / screening / gravitational-wave phenomenology
- **Status:** accepted in *Physical Review D* on 2026-09-08; peer-reviewed-accepted, not yet version-of-record at the time of this watch
- **Source quality:** high for the stated model construction and accepted-paper conclusions; medium for broad phenomenological extrapolation because the result is model-specific and does not yet constitute an observational detection

## Accepted result
Farid Thaalba, Leonardo Gualtieri, Thomas P. Sotiriou, and Enrico Trincherini study shift-symmetric scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravity extended by a cubic Galileon interaction with a distinct energy scale. The extra scale changes the near-horizon scalar dynamics enough to generate a screening mechanism that suppresses the scalar charge and associated dipolar emission. In the accepted-paper summary, this permits substantially larger Gauss–Bonnet couplings to remain compatible with existing gravitational-wave bounds than in the corresponding single-scale setup. The same hierarchy also allows smaller black holes than the ordinary branch and shifts strong-field observables, with the scalar charge, ISCO and ISCO frequency most affected for small black holes.

## Why it matters
This changes the interpretation of scalar–Gauss–Bonnet constraints: dipole-radiation limits cannot automatically be promoted into scale-independent exclusions once technically natural derivative interactions are added. A multi-scale completion can screen the scalar channel precisely where binary-GW bounds are strongest while retaining non-GR strong-field structure elsewhere. The practical consequence is a model-selection rule: constraints on sGB couplings should be reported together with assumptions about derivative self-interactions and screening scales, and searches should include observables that survive screening, especially compact-object structure and orbital/ISCO diagnostics.

This is distinct from the previously indexed Galileon EFT well-posedness result: that work addressed nonlinear evolution and EFT breakdown, whereas this result is a concrete strong-field screening mechanism inside a two-scale scalar–Gauss–Bonnet model that reshapes the mapping between GW dipole bounds and allowed coupling strength.
