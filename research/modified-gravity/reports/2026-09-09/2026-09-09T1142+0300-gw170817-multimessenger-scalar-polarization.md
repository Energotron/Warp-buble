# GW170817 multimessenger geometry tightens scalar-polarization tests

- **Accepted:** 2026-09-09 11:42 +03:00
- **Classification:** observation / interpretation / multimessenger gravitational-wave test / scalar polarization / parametrized modified gravity
- **Source quality:** high for the published analysis and quoted parameter constraints (peer-reviewed *Physical Review D*); medium for any modified-gravity interpretation because the analysis is phenomenological/PPE rather than a fit to one complete underlying gravity theory, and Bayesian model comparison still favors GR.
- **Status:** accepted as a significant new observational-strategy result. The reported 2–3σ scalar-amplitude offset is **not** treated as evidence for modified gravity because the extra-parameter model loses under Bayesian evidence.

## Result

Imam & Lagos analyze GW170817 with an extended parametrized post-Einsteinian waveform containing a scalar breathing polarization plus tensor-mode modifications. The new ingredient is the electromagnetic constraint on the source polarization angle inferred from the long-term gamma-ray-burst afterglow, combined with the other counterpart information.

For the quadrupolar `l=|m|=2` sector, the scalar-amplitude posterior can sit about 2–3σ away from zero inside the extended model. However, model selection still favors pure GR once the additional parameters are penalized, with a reported `Δ log Z = 4.67 ± 0.32` in favor of GR. The robust methodological result is stronger: adding the EM polarization-angle information improves the bound on the scalar-amplitude modification by roughly **60%** and the tensor-amplitude modification by roughly **30%** in the cited quadrupolar case.

## Why this changes the picture

This upgrades multimessenger follow-up from merely supplying sky position, distance/redshift, or inclination information to providing a direct **geometric prior on GW polarization content**. Extra-polarization searches are often limited by degeneracies among inclination, polarization angle and non-GR amplitudes; an independently constrained afterglow geometry can remove a substantial part of that degeneracy.

For modified-gravity observational strategy, the implication is concrete: future binary-neutron-star tests of scalar/vector polarizations should preserve and propagate late-time jet/afterglow polarization-angle information into the GW inference pipeline rather than treating EM follow-up only as localization/redshift metadata.

The 2–3σ scalar offset should not be promoted as a discovery claim. The Bayesian evidence result shows why posterior offsets inside a flexible beyond-GR model must be separated from model-level evidence.

## Provenance-linked QuantDeus roadmap note

**Modified-gravity observation rule:** for future multimessenger compact-binary tests, treat EM-constrained source geometry—especially polarization angle from long-term afterglow imaging/polarimetry—as a first-class prior for extra GW polarizations. In GW170817 the added angle information tightened the scalar-amplitude bound by ~60% in the tested quadrupolar PPE case, while Bayesian evidence still favored GR; keep parameter-level anomalies separate from model-selection claims.
