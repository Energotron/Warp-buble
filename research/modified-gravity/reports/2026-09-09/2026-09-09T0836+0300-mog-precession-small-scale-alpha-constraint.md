# MOG small-scale precession constraint from binary pulsars and Solar-System data

- **Accepted:** 2026-09-09 08:36 +03:00
- **Classification:** observation-facing model test / weak-field constraint / scalar-tensor-vector gravity (MOG/STVG) / orbital precession
- **Source quality:** high for the published calculation and stated bounds (peer-reviewed *Physics Letters B*); medium for theory-wide interpretation because the paper constrains the Schwarzschild–MOG exterior with a single effective coupling `alpha`, whereas broader STVG phenomenology may allow scale/environment dependence.
- **Status:** accepted as a significant new constraint on the universal/small-scale Schwarzschild–MOG branch; not interpreted as a blanket exclusion of every scale-dependent STVG realization.

## Result

Yang, Li, Jiang & Lin derive the first post-Newtonian periastron precession of timelike orbits in the Schwarzschild–MOG exterior and confront it with five binary-pulsar systems plus Solar-System measurements. They report bounds on the dimensionless MOG coupling at roughly `alpha ~ 10^-6–10^-5` on these small scales.

This is qualitatively stronger than earlier S2/OJ 287 bounds at `alpha ~ 10^-1`, and is radically below the often quoted galaxy-rotation-curve best-fit value `alpha ≈ 8.9` when `alpha` is treated as universal. Therefore a scale-independent Schwarzschild–MOG coupling cannot simply be transported from galactic fits into precision weak-field systems; viable MOG/STVG implementations must either exhibit strong running/screening/environment dependence or alter the effective exterior model used in these tests.

## Why this changes the picture

The key update is not merely another orbital constraint. It sharpens a cross-scale consistency problem: the same parameterization that can require order-unity-to-large effective coupling on galactic scales is forced to nearly zero by precision small-scale precession. That makes **scale dependence itself** a primary falsifiable ingredient of MOG/STVG rather than an optional model detail.

For modified-gravity model selection, future comparisons should therefore avoid quoting a single global `alpha` without demonstrating how the theory maps between Solar-System, binary-pulsar, black-hole and galactic environments.
